# Eco Town of the Future: plan for three afternoon modules

*The user's plan, as given on 15 November 2026. Kept word for word. The shared contract it asks for is in `docs/MODULES.md`.*

## 1. Decisions made by the user (15 Nov 2026)

- The full-day camp is **Eco Town in the morning, "Eco Town of the future" in the afternoon**.
- The morning hour (the existing lab) keeps the lesson "AI can be confidently wrong; check it". **That theme stops there.** It is not carried into the afternoon.
- The afternoon is **freer play and learning to work with AI and machines**: real, transferable skills, inside the Eco Town world.
- **Variety matters.** Not everything on the island from above. Each module has its own viewpoint and kind of play.
- **Parents are sold skills, and must be able to see what their child made.** Each module produces something for a take-home record.
- **Three new modules, built in parallel by three agents, one each:** drone delivery, robot training, roads for self-driving cars.
- This **replaces the brief's instruction not to build modules 2 to 6 yet**. Update `docs/pilot-plan.md` and the root `CLAUDE.md` to say so.

## 3. The shared module contract (main agent writes this first)

One small loader and one interface, so the three agents add files and never edit the large shared ones.

- **Where a module lives:** its own folder, e.g. `games/ecotown/game/js/modules/<id>/`, with its own stylesheet and its own tests. Ids: `drone`, `robots`, `roads`.
- **How it opens:** from the town (a building or menu entry) and by address (`?module=<id>`), through the loader. It always has one marked way back to the town.
- **What it is given:** the child's own town (read-only cells and simulation result), the age route, the settings (read-aloud, reduced motion, large text), Florence and the 3D engine.
- **What it reports:** progress and finish in the same status shape the leader's list uses (`module`, `stage`, `state`, `finished`), saved through the existing save and server copy.
- **What it hands back:** a small record for the take-home page: what the child made, and two to four "You practised…" lines built from what they did.
- **What it may change in the town:** one visible result (a drone route, a working robot, a rebuilt street), applied through the loader, never by writing the save directly.

## 4. Rules for all three agents

- Work in a **separate working copy** each. Add new files; do not edit `game.js`, `main.js`, `game.css`, `content.js` or anything under `worker/`. If a shared change is needed, ask the main agent.
- **Nothing a child types leaves the device. No names. No free text generated by Florence:** she assembles from built-in choices, as with the word tiles.
- **No pass or fail lessons about catching the AI.** Florence is a capable partner here. Machines may still have honest limits the child can see and fix.
- Plain British English, short sentences, key instructions spoken as well as written. No budget or coins for ages 8–12.
- **Never deploy.** Never touch the original sites.
- Works on a laptop and a tablet, by touch and by keyboard, with reduced motion respected.
- **First version only: one playable loop of about ten minutes**, with unit tests for its logic and one browser run that plays it start to finish without errors.

## 5. The three modules

### Drone delivery
- **View and play:** flying through the child's own town, from behind the drone. Very forgiving controls: fun within a minute.
- **Loop:** fly one delivery by hand; then plan the route on a map and write the drone's instructions from tiles; watch it fly itself; adjust and compare with the hand-flown run.
- **Skill practised:** planning a route and breaking it into steps a machine can follow; seeing what to automate.
- **Back to the town:** a drone route that keeps flying in their town.
- **Take-home:** the route map and the instructions they wrote.

### Robot training
- **View and play:** indoors at a workshop bench, then watching the robot at street level in their town.
- **Loop:** show a recycling or repair robot examples; test it; see where it fails; add the examples it lacked; test again.
- **Skill practised:** teaching by example and testing, which is how machine learning works.
- **Back to the town:** the trained robot works in their streets.
- **Take-home:** the robot's training record: what it was shown and how its results changed.

### Roads for self-driving cars
- **View and play:** a close-up street and junction view, then a ride along it.
- **Loop:** redesign one street for shared self-driving cars: drop-off points, waiting bays, crossings for people walking and cycling; run it; decide what to do with the land the car park no longer needs.
- **Skill practised:** designing a system for a machine user and weighing a trade-off with real numbers.
- **Back to the town:** the rebuilt street and the freed land.
- **Take-home:** the street before and after, with what was gained.

## 6. After the three first versions

1. Main agent reviews each, merges them one at a time, and runs the whole test suite and both play-throughs after each merge.
2. **Take-home record:** one printable page per child for the day: a picture of their town, what they made in each module, the "You practised…" lines, and one question to ask at home. Printed or saved on the device; nothing sent anywhere; a line to write the name by hand. It says what the child made and practised, never what they learned or how much they improved.
3. A person plays each module. Agents can show that a drone flies and a loop completes; they cannot judge whether it is fun for a nine-year-old.
4. Only then decide which modules to grow to full length.
