Proposal · how the redesigned game plays

Ask, look, decide

There is no free building. In every level of every town the child does the same three things: asks Florence with a sentence built from word buttons, looks through lenses at the outcome of each solution she proposes, and chooses one. Florence is never set up to be wrong and never says how sure she is. What changes from level to level is how much the child does alone.

1 · One level

2 · From the first level to the last

Every town has six levels and climbs the same ladder. Level 1 is nearly all Florence; level 6 is nearly all the child.

3 · Every town, level by level

The level titles are the missions already approved in the curriculum. Under each is what Florence's solutions are about in that level.

4 · Across the day

Florence is the same all day

In every town she proposes different solutions to the job. All of them would work. She is not there to be caught out, and there is no "how sure" figure anywhere.

Two kinds of solution

Design: where it goes and what it is made of, such as a route, a street layout or a robot's body. Operating: how it is run, such as a rule set, a timetable or an order of work. Each town has both.

The last level is the whole world

Power Ridge feeds every town, so Storm night is decided on the whole map: who gets power back first, and what that means for each town.

What this changes

  • Eco Town's old lesson goes. "AI can be confidently wrong; check it" and the percentage beside Florence's plans are not carried over. Eco Town plays like the other five towns.
  • Eco Town loses its free building. Today a child builds a town of their own between missions. Here the six missions are the hour, and the town grows from the scenarios the child chooses.
  • Hands-on levels become judging levels. Hand flying, labelling scenes and editing are things a child does by hand in the approved curriculum. In this flow Florence proposes ways of doing them and the child judges, unless a short hands-on step is kept inside those levels.
  • The town shows the result. The chosen scenario plays out in the 3D town, so the world from the map proposal fills in as the day goes on.

Decisions for you

  1. Keep a hands-on step? For example, after choosing a flight plan the child flies its last ten seconds. It keeps the real skill but is a small piece of play outside the loop.
  2. Can a child choose a weak scenario? I would let them, and show what happens, then offer "Ask again". The other way is to block it until the lenses pass.
  3. Is the ladder the same in every town, or does each later town start a step higher because the child has already learnt the loop?
