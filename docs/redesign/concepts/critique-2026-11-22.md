# Independent critique of the seven play-break concept screens (22 November 2026)

An independent game designer reviewed the seven screens in `screens/` from the still images only. This is a faithful summary of that report.

## Verdict

The set is a real step away from the scripted game, but only four of the seven are truly different activities. Land, Factory and School are the same activity: pick from a list and drag to a yellow place. That is following instructions, not a puzzle. The walk and the farm carry the biggest promise of a new camera and are the two weakest pictures.

## Screen by screen

**1. Land map (4.5 out of 10).** From above, a home is an orange striped rectangle and does not read as a house. Pale white squares are unexplained. "5 of 12" does not say twelve of what. Yellow is close to the sand colour of the river bank. Twelve identical homes is a chore after the third. The edges are full of city blocks and roads that are not part of the task. It is the same patch of land as before, photographed from above, so "a larger area of the world" is not answered. Fixes: pull the camera back so the child sees a wider world (hills, whole river, coast, the city far off); offer more yellow places than parts so the child decides; give each building a face that reads from above (chimney, door step, garden, roof colour); a bounce and a person walking out when it lands.

**2. Paths map (6.5).** The clearest instruction and the best action (drawing). The single bridge is the only real decision in the set. The finished path is the same colour as the river bank. "5 of 11" is unclear. Path has no number so the child does not know when it is finished. Unclear whether the path may cross the river without the bridge. Holding the button is hard on a trackpad: allow click to click as well, and an easy way to rub out. Fixes: each home visibly wakes up when the path reaches it (door opens, a person steps out); give the path its own colour; replace the counter with a row of small houses that light up one by one.

**3. Walk (3).** No destination, nothing to collect, nothing to find. One dark tree fills about 40 per cent of the screen and one roof about 30 per cent. The avatar is two plain boxes with no face, arms or legs, and two other box people hide the path. Roofs look like staircases at this height. Free steering in 3D will get children stuck. Fixes: put the walk on rails along the path the child drew (hold one key to go forward), with the camera higher and further back, over the shoulder, looking down the street, so the child sees their homes, bridge and benches coming up; give it a one-minute purpose (wave to each neighbour, or deliver a letter to each door, and finish by crossing the child's own bridge); give the avatar a face and legs.

**4. Factory (6).** The most attractive picture. Two dark roof beams cross the front and cut through the building pad and the cursor. The yellow target is about the size of the cursor. The diagram is a small board on the back wall, half hidden, and cannot be read. "1 of 6" is unclear. The order is fixed and each part has one place, so there is nothing to think about. It does not show the plan the child chose. Fixes: remove or fade the front beams; zoom in so the home fills a third of the screen; make the diagram a large picture beside the parts list in which each part lights up as it is placed; build one home with care and one free choice (roof or door colour); show it driving out on the lorry.

**5. Farm (5).** Children know this kind of game at once. The collectables are yellow squares that look like coins, which the game does not have; nothing looks like food. It is hard to tell which lines can be stood on. The blue column is unexplained. Both an up arrow and "Space to jump" are shown. The character is tiny, the middle band is an empty wall, and the only link between greenhouse and tunnel is one very long ladder. Nothing says hydroponics: no water, roots or pipes. A missed jump can leave a child stuck. Fixes: make the food the collectable (recognisable tomato, lettuce, strawberry that pops into a basket on the character's back, leaving the tray visibly empty); show water pipes and roots; zoom in so the character is two or three times larger and the camera follows; replace the ladder with a lift the child rides; cut the empty building out; every gap must be passable by walking, with jumping only for extras.

**6. Control room (5.5).** The big glowing numbers and small models are good. The numbers do not add up to anything a child can see (1, 6 and 5, but the tank says 6 of 10). No visible target for what the town needs. Nothing says the row of lamps is a clock. The season keys are four coloured squares with one label. The red button is unexplained. A box person hides part of the desk and the bottom third is empty floor. The window is dim and the lit windows of the homes are tiny. The room is too dark for a bright classroom. It has the most potential because saving and releasing power is a real small game. Fixes: one thing to watch and one thing to do: show the town's need as a row of ten house-shaped lights, show power arriving as glowing blocks that slide from each station into that row, and make the battery one big lever with "save" and "use"; make the window the reward: brighter, closer, homes go dark or light up street by street, and the sun crosses the sky in the window so the time of day needs no label.

**7. School (7).** The strongest. The child in the wheelchair waiting at the steps tells the problem without words. The yellow place for the ramp is faint and half hidden. A see-through block at the right end reads as a rendering fault. A dark green rectangle on the lawn may be a place or a shadow. The parts panel covers the right end of the building. The hint describes the screen and does not say what to do. Fixes: every part has a person waiting for it and a short scene when it is placed (a child with a walking frame at the stairs for the lift, a child covering their ears for the quiet room); two possible places for at least two parts; far brighter yellow places with a soft pulse.

## Across all seven

- **Variety.** There are four activities: placing (1, 4, 7), drawing (2), moving a little person (3, 5), operating a machine (6). Keep list-and-place for the factory and school. Make the land map more like the path map, so the child paints or stamps directly on the land.
- **Making it a real puzzle without failure.** (1) More places than parts. (2) Two good places with different visible results, and the child may move the part once; nobody says "wrong". (3) One scarce part, like the single bridge: one lift and two shafts, three parks and five neighbourhoods. (4) The part that matches the child's own plan: a thick wall and a thin wall for a warm home; a worker shivers and holds up the thick one as a hint. (5) A choice of taste: roof colour, door colour, kind of tree.
- **Pacing.** One break of two to three minutes per level, sitting exactly where Florence builds now. Level 2 is the exception: about ninety seconds on the map and one minute for the walk. The level ends on the usual high view with the new thing in it. After about three minutes Florence offers to finish, and the child loses nothing.
- **Showing a friend.** Every child's town is identical at present. Missing: visible differences the child chose; continuity between breaks (the walk passes the homes placed in level 1, the factory home arrives in the town, the control room window shows the child's own town with their bridge); people who react (come out, wave, use the ramp, sit on the bench); a keepsake (a photo of the town at the end of each level, a one-minute tour at the end of the hour).

## The three most important fixes

1. Give the placing breaks a real choice and a personal mark.
2. Rebuild the two avatar breaks, the walk and the farm.
3. Make the control room readable.

Also deal early with the land map not showing a larger world, because the owner asked for that directly.
