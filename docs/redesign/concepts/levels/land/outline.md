# Level 1, Land: outline for the owner

This is an outline only. Nothing has been built. Any words quoted here that are not in the approved wireframes are placeholders.

## The place

The level happens in an empty river valley, before Eco Town exists. There are no streets and no buildings. There is a wide river, a raised bank beside it, a low flat meadow further back, and a hill behind the meadow. The family has come to look for land, with rucksacks and raincoats, and they stand with the child on a small wooden footbridge. Homes is a neat, built street in sunshine. This level is open, wild country with a big sky, water and reeds, and nothing man-made except the bridge and what the child adds.

## The camera, the time of day and the weather

The camera is low on the footbridge, at the family's shoulder, looking across the water. From there the three places sit one behind another in a single picture: the bank is near, the meadow is in the middle and the hill is at the back. It is late afternoon, and dark rain clouds are building over the hill while low sunlight still comes in from the side. After the test the clouds part and the wet land shines in evening light.

## The test: "Make it rain"

1. When Florence shows her three plans, a group of three small wooden test huts appears on each place. Every hut has a lit lantern on a pole, and each group has a name tag.
2. The child presses "Make it rain". The clouds close over, rain falls, and the lanterns glow in the grey light.
3. A striped measuring post stands in the river beside the bridge. The river climbs the post one stripe at a time, in three slow steps, so the child can see how high it is.
4. At the second stripe, water spreads across the meadow. It reaches the huts there and their lanterns go out one by one.
5. At the third stripe, the river pulls blocks out of the bank. The huts on the bank stay above the water, but one of them tips over at the edge.
6. The huts on the hill do nothing at all. Their lanterns stay lit and a path of dry grass can be seen around them.
7. The rain stops and the river stays high for the result cards. At a glance the child sees dark huts standing in water on the meadow, a tipped hut on a broken bank, and three upright, lit huts on the hill. The cards then give the approved sentence for each plan, with the tick, dash or cross after it.

Nobody stands in the places being tested. The family watches from the bridge under an umbrella, so nothing here suggests going near flood water.

## The play break: mark out the town

1. The camera rises until it looks straight down on the chosen land, like a map that is the real ground. The river has gone down, but it has left a line of mud and driftwood that shows how high the water came.
2. The helper panel holds ten home plots and one town green. The land shows about sixteen yellow places, so there are more places than homes.
3. The child selects a plot in the panel, the free places light up, and the child clicks or drags to press it into the ground. A pegged outline and a small block home appear, and a family member walks over to it.
4. The child places the town green and chooses the colour of the town flag that stands on it.
5. The camera drops back to the bridge, and the child sees the new town in its place across the water.

The child's hands do what a town planner does on the first day, which is to decide where things go by reading the land. The high water line teaches the idea of the level: the land remembers where the river has been, and a child can see whether each home sits above or below that line.

## The mark on the town

The whole town stands where the child put it for the other five levels, with the child's own arrangement of homes, the green and the flag. If the child presses "Try another plan", the same ten homes move to the new place and the rain falls on them, so "What changed?" is about the child's own homes.

## How long it takes

The level should take about ten minutes. The test takes about forty seconds and the play break about three minutes.

## The two biggest risks

The first risk is the picture. Three places must be told apart in one low view, and rising water made of blocks must look good. If the far hill is too small to see, the camera will have to move from place to place, which makes the answer slower to see.

The second risk is the play break. Placing homes on a map could feel like arranging things and not like learning, and it could look like the placing in another level. The high water line has to be strong enough in the picture to keep the activity about the river.
