# Level 6, "A school for everyone": outline of the place and the activity

## The place, and why it does not look like Homes

The place is the whole valley of Eco Town seen as one long strip from the far bank of the river. The town and its clock tower are at the left end, the park is at the edge of the town, an open meadow lies in the middle, and the farm houses and barns are at the right end. One lane joins them all. Homes is a closed street that runs away from the camera under trees. This level is open country that runs across the screen from left to right, so the child can see "near" and "far" as a plain distance along one line. It is autumn, with orange and red trees, hay bales and bright leaves lying on the lane.

## The camera, the time of day and the weather

The camera sits low on the opposite river bank, at the height of a child, with reeds and a duck in front of it. It moves sideways along the bank and never rises above the roofs. The time is school time on a bright, crisp morning, at about half past eight. The sun is well up, the sky is high, clear and deep blue, and the shadows are sharp. A light breeze carries red and orange leaves across the lane and turns the weathercock on the clock tower, and there is no mist. During the play break the camera crosses the river and travels beside the children at their shoulder height, as a film camera travels beside people who are walking.

## What happens when the child presses "Ring the school bell"

1. The three places for the school are marked on the strip by three small flags. The test runs once for each plan, for about eight seconds each, in the same view.
2. The school of the first plan rises at its place. The bell swings in its small tower, and a golden ring of sound spreads out along the valley.
3. When the ring reaches a home, the front door opens and a child steps out with a school bag. There are twelve children, six in the town and six by the farms.
4. Children who live near enough set off on foot along the lane, in twos and threes, and they are bright and large. At homes that are too far away, a grey car backs out instead, joins a slow queue on the lane and leaves a grey puff of pollution behind it.
5. Everyone arrives at the school gate. Each child hangs a badge on a board at the gate. The badge shows a shoe if the child walked and a car if the child was driven.
6. The view shrinks into a thin strip that stays on the screen, and the next plan plays.

At the end, the three strips sit one above the other, and each result card shows its row of twelve badges before the tick, dash or cross. The school in the town centre shows six shoes and six cars. The school beside the park shows nine shoes and three cars. The school between the town and the farms shows twelve shoes and an empty lane. The answer is in the shapes of the badges and in the queue of cars, and not in colour alone.

## The play break: lead the walking bus

A walking bus is a real thing that schools do. A teacher and a line of children walk to school together and pick up friends on the way. The child leads one, with a teacher at the front and the child's friend from the farms as the first passenger.

1. The child chooses the colour of the flag and of the bright vests. This is the child's personal mark.
2. The bus sets off along the lane. A friend waits at each garden gate with a parent, and a yellow ring pulses around them. The child clicks the friend, the friend waves goodbye and joins the end of the line, and the line grows longer.
3. At one fork the child chooses between two good ways. One way goes through the orchard, and the other goes over the footbridge by the duck pond. Each way shows different things, and neither is wrong.
4. Where the lane crosses the road, the child clicks the button on the crossing. The bus waits for the green figure and then crosses with the teacher.
5. At the school gate the bus from the other end of the valley arrives at the same moment, and the two lines meet. Every child hangs a shoe badge on the board.
6. The child drags the bell rope down with the mouse, and the bell rings over the valley.

The child's hands click to gather friends, click to choose a way, click to cross safely and drag to ring the bell. The activity teaches that a school which is near enough lets children arrive on their own feet, together, and that walking is the cheerful way to arrive. The length of the bus follows the plan the child chose. With the school between the town and the farms, all six farm children join. With the school beside the park, three join and three arrive by car at the gate. With the school in the town centre, the child leads the short bus from the town, and the farm children step out of a queue of cars. Nobody is told off, and the child simply sees it.

## The mark the choice leaves on the town

The school stands at the place the child chose, with the child's flag colour on its bell tower. The walking bus stops stay along the lane as small signposts in the same colour, and the trodden path to the school stays visible. In every later view of the town at morning, a line of children in the child's vest colour walks to school. If the child chose another plan, a grey bay for cars stays outside the gate instead.

## How long it should take

The whole level should take about ten minutes. The test takes about thirty seconds. The play break takes two and a half to three minutes, and after three minutes Florence offers to walk the rest of the way, so that the child loses nothing.

## The two biggest risks

The first risk is that the play break looks too much like the walk in the "Getting around" level, because both show people on a path. I will keep it distinct by making it about gathering friends into one growing line that ends at a bell, with a camera that travels beside the line and not behind it, on a bright morning of blowing autumn leaves. If the other outline also gathers people along a route, one of us should change.

The second risk is that the long view across the river makes the children too small to read, and that three test runs in a row ask the child to remember the first one. I will make the figures large for the distance, keep the lane close to the near bank, and keep each finished run on the screen as a strip with its row of badges, so that the comparison never depends on memory.
