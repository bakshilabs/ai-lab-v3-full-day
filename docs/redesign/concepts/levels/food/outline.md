# Eco Town, level 4, "Food": outline for the owner

This is an outline only. Nothing has been built. It follows the approved wireframe: the sentence, the word buttons, Florence's three plans, the two checks ("Nature" and "Fresh food"), the test button "Take the food to market" and the recap stay as they are.

## The place, and why it does not look like Homes

The level happens outside the town, in the wild meadow beside the lane that leads to the market gate. Homes is a street of buildings and trees seen by a person standing up. Food is open country: tall grass and flowers in the front of the picture, a lane with a low hedge, open fields behind, and the market awnings and the town roofs small in the distance. There are almost no buildings. The colours are greens, poppy red, cornflower blue and the pale gold of early light, and not the warm brick and timber of the street.

## The camera, the time of day and the weather

The camera is at the height of a frog, down among the flowers, looking across the meadow to the lane and the market. Bees fly close to the camera, a frog sits on a stone beside a small pond, and birds are in the hedge. It is dawn at the end of summer. There is low mist on the fields, dew on the grass, long shadows and no wind. The sun rises during the level, and its height in the sky is how the child sees time pass. There is no clock and no timer.

## What happens when the child presses "Take the food to market"

One press plays the three plans one after the other, each for about twelve seconds, from the same spot in the meadow.

1. For the farms on the wild meadow, the flowers around the camera are ploughed into rows of cabbages, the pond is filled in, the bees fly away and the frog hops out of the picture. A farmer and a child then take a cart of loose vegetables the short way to the market while the sun is still low.
2. For the farms on open land close to town, the meadow stays as it was. The bees keep working and the frog stays on its stone. The same cart rolls past along the lane from the fields behind and reaches the market while the sun is still low.
3. For the farms on open land far away, the meadow also stays, but no cart comes. The sun climbs to the middle of the sky. A lorry then arrives on the far road, and people carry sealed cold boxes to the stall.

The picture then becomes three panels side by side, one for each plan, and each panel holds two things only: the meadow in front, which is alive or bare, and the market stall behind, which shows open crates under a low sun or cold boxes under a high sun. That is the answer at a glance. The result cards sit under the panels, with each sentence first and its tick or dash after it.

## The play break: "Harvest morning"

The camera moves to the child's own farm bed and looks along the rows from kneeling height, with the family beside the cart. On the meadow plan and the close plan the steps are these.

1. The child picks three crops from five. This is the personal mark.
2. The child clicks plots to plant them. There are more plots than seeds, so the child decides where each crop goes, and the plots left over become a strip of flowers.
3. The plants grow, and bees come across from the meadow. The child clicks each bee to lead it to a flower, and a fruit grows where the bee has been. On the meadow plan fewer bees come.
4. The child clicks the ripe food to pick it, and drags each crate on to the cart.
5. The child rides to the market with the family and clicks to set the crates out on the stall.

If the child chose the far plan, steps 4 and 5 become packing cold boxes and loading the lorry, and the stall is set out at midday. The child's hands plant, lead bees, pick, load and set out. The activity teaches that food grown close can go from the ground to the stall in one morning, and that the wild place beside the farm helps the food to grow.

## The mark on the town

The town keeps the farm with the child's three crops in the rows and places they chose, the flower strip, the meadow as the plan left it, and a stall at the market with the child's food on it. A cart or a lorry makes the journey every morning.

## How long it should take

The whole level should take about ten minutes. The test takes about forty seconds, and the play break takes three to four minutes.

## The two biggest risks

1. The frog's view could hide the journey behind grass, and three plans shown one after the other could feel slow. The grass must frame the lane without covering it, and the three panels at the end must carry the answer by themselves.
2. The play break could turn into a clicking chore or a decorating game. It needs few plots and few crates, and the bees must stay at the centre of it so that it remains about the idea of the level.

One question is open for the owner. In the wireframe the second word button is about how many trees and flowers to keep, but the three plans do not yet differ by it. I suggest that the child's answer sets how wide the hedge and the flower edge around the farm are in every plan, so that the request visibly changes the result.
