# Eco Town, level 4, "Food": second outline for the owner

This is an outline only. Nothing has been built. It replaces the first outline, which was about farms against wild places. The owner's new direction is that the level compares distant industrial farming, high-tech urban farming and urban community farms. Because the approved wireframe no longer fits, this outline also proposes new words. Every proposed word below is a placeholder for the owner to judge.

## The place, and why it does not look like the other levels

The picture is three long slices of the world, stacked like three shelves and seen from the side at the height of the people in them. The top slice is the distant farm: huge flat fields, one big harvester, a packing shed and a long road. The middle slice is a cutaway of an indoor farm in the town, with shelves of plants on three floors under pink and violet growing lights. The bottom slice is a community garden between two rows of homes, with raised beds, a rain barrel and neighbours of all ages. All three slices end on the right at the same market hall. No other level uses a side view in slices, and no other level has the glow of the growing lights.

## The camera, the time of day and the weather

The camera looks straight at the slices from the side and slides to the right to follow the food. The level has no single season. It starts on a midsummer morning, and during the test the whole year turns, so the weather is the year itself: summer sun, autumn leaves, snow, and spring rain.

## Proposed words (placeholders)

The job on the first screen is: "The town needs fresh fruit and vegetables all year. Ask Florence to suggest plans for growing them. Then test her plans, so that you can make a good decision."

The scene line is: "Eco Town has cold winters, and fresh food does not keep for long. How could the town grow it?"

The sentence is: "Plan how to grow fresh fruit and vegetables that reach the market quickly, all year round. Grow them" followed by the first gap, "and grow them" followed by the second gap. The first gap offers "far away, where there is lots of land", "at the edge of the town" and "inside the town". The second gap offers "outdoors in the soil", "under glass" and "indoors under lights". The goal is fixed in the sentence. A child can reason that food grown inside the town reaches the market soonest, and that cold winters make growing indoors the surest way to have food all year.

The three plans are "A big farm far away" (huge fields and machines grow the food, and lorries bring it to the town), "An indoor farm in the town" (plants grow on shelves indoors, in water and under lights) and "Community gardens in the town" (neighbours grow food together in gardens between the homes).

The two checks are "Short journey" and "Food all year".

For the big farm far away, the result for "Short journey" is "The food was picked days ago, kept cold and driven a long way. The lorries caused pollution.", with a cross. Its result for "Food all year" is "The big fields sent plenty of food in every season.", with a tick.

For the indoor farm, the result for "Short journey" is "The food was picked this morning and came two streets by cargo bike.", with a tick. Its result for "Food all year" is "The indoor farm grew salad, tomatoes and strawberries in every season. Its lights use electricity.", with a tick. This is the plan that is good on every check, and the sample child chooses it.

For the community gardens, the result for "Short journey" is "The food was picked this morning and walked to the market on a handcart.", with a tick. Its result for "Food all year" is "The gardens were full in summer and autumn. In winter there was little to pick.", with a dash.

The recap tap is "Point to the place that still grew fresh food in the snow.", and the answer is the indoor farm. It never blocks progress.

I believe this is fair to each kind of farming. The big farm grows the most food and never runs short, but its food travels far. The community gardens give the freshest food and bring neighbours together, which the picture shows, but they rest in winter. The indoor farm is close and grows all year, but it needs electricity and it grows salad and soft fruit, not wheat or potatoes. That is why the job speaks only of fresh fruit and vegetables.

## The test button, and what the child sees

"Take the food to market" still fits, and I suggest keeping it. One press sends food from all three places at the same moment, twice: once in summer and once in winter, with autumn passing between them. The whole test takes about forty seconds.

1. In summer the cargo bike and the handcart reach the market almost at once, with open crates. The lorry is still on the road, with grey pollution behind it, and arrives last with sealed cold boxes.
2. The leaves turn and the snow falls. The garden beds go white and the indoor farm glows as before.
3. In winter the cargo bike arrives with full crates. The handcart arrives with one small basket. The lorry arrives last again, with plenty of boxes.

The camera then rests on three market stalls side by side, each with a summer shelf and a winter shelf. Full crates, an almost empty winter shelf and a pile of cold boxes give the answer at a glance. The result cards sit below, with each sentence before its mark.

## The play break: "Grow a crate and take it to market"

The camera moves inside the slice the child chose. For the indoor farm the steps are these.

1. The child picks three crops from five. This is the personal mark.
2. The child clicks shelves to slot in trays of seedlings. There are more places than trays, and the tall tomato plants only fit the tall shelves.
3. The child turns the water valve. Water runs through clear pipes past white roots, and there is no soil.
4. The child switches on the lights. Snow falls outside the window and the plants grow anyway.
5. The child clicks the ripe food into a crate, rides the lift down with the grower, loads the cargo bike and sets out the stall.

The other two plans use the same five steps with different things: beds, a watering can from the rain barrel and a handcart in the garden, and rows, the harvester and cold boxes on the big farm. The activity teaches that plants need water, light and food, that they do not always need soil or summer, and that stacking shelves grows a lot on little land.

## The mark on the town

The town keeps what the child made. For the indoor farm this is a glass farm building that glows in the evening with the child's three crops on its floors, a cargo bike on its round and the child's stall in the market. The other plans leave gardens between the homes, or lorries on the road each morning.

## How long it should take

The whole level should take about ten minutes, and the play break three to four minutes of that.

## The two biggest risks

1. Three slices make each one small on the screen, and the indoor farm could look cold beside the friendly garden. The chosen slice must fill the screen in the play break, and the indoor farm needs people, green leaves and warm light as well as pink light.
2. The play break has three versions, which is three times the building. I would build the indoor farm fully first and keep the other two shorter.

## Two questions for the owner

Florence always offers the same three plans, so the child's words do not yet change the result. I suggest that the child's two words decide which plan Florence shows first and describes as the nearest to the request. The owner may also want a third check, "Growing together", so that the strength of the community gardens earns a tick of its own.
