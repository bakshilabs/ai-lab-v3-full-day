Eco Town: leader's one-hour run sheet

Children build a town with Florence, an AI planner who sounds sure and is sometimes wrong. The aim of the hour: every child checks an AI's plan against evidence, and fixes it with a clearer instruction. You do not need to know the game. Read this page once. To print it, use your browser's Print.

The hour

AboutWhat happens
0–5 minStart. Each child types the group's code, sees their badge name (for example "Heron 47"), picks the age route you chose and names a town. Florence shows the first three taps.
5–20Build, then the first two missions. The AI Lab button shows a number when a mission is ready. In each mission: tell Florence what to plan, check her plan with a lens, fix it.
20–25Pause 1. After the second mission the game shows a "Pause and talk" card. Screens down. Ask the question on the card.
25–45Missions three and four, with building in between as the town grows.
45–50Pause 2. The card appears after the fourth mission.
50–60Missions five and six if there is time, then finish. Menu → Finish session shows each child what they practised, their choices and their town. End on its two "Talk about it" questions.

The times are a guide. Nothing in the game waits for a clock: a pause card appears when a child reaches it, and Carry on closes it. A child who has not done all six missions still gets a full recap. This timing has not yet been tried with a group of children.

Before the children arrive

  • Open the game on every device and check the island appears. Bring headphones, or agree sound off: Florence reads instructions aloud, and many children will not read them.
  • Write the group's code where everyone can see it.
  • On the start screen choose "Ages 8–12" or "Ages 13–17" for the whole group. In session setup choose In order so everyone meets the missions in the same order, and tick Facilitator notes. Either one turns the pause cards on.
  • Open the leader's page on your own device (your leader code). It lists each badge, the step they are on and who needs help.

Where the game makes a child wait

  • Builds open as the town grows: homes after 6 tiles of town land, food at 12 families, power at 24, the town centre at 36. A child who says "it's locked" needs more homes, not help with the buttons.
  • Some builds must be repeated (several homes, several farms). The line at the top left always names the next step and shows how far along it is.
  • A refresh is safe. The town is saved; a mission in progress offers to start again.

Questions to ask
  • Pause 1: "Florence was very sure. Why did her plan still go wrong?"
  • Pause 2: "Which check changed your mind about a plan?"
  • Any time: "What did Florence guess because you did not tell her?"
  • If someone finishes early: "Try an instruction with a number in it. Did the plan meet your number?"

Common mistakes, and how to help without telling

  • Accepting the plan unchecked. Ask: "How could you find out if she is right?" Point at the lens buttons, not at one of them.
  • Rewriting the same instruction. Florence says what still fails and how she read the words. Ask the child to read that line aloud, then: "Which word did she take differently from you?"
  • "It says partly right." The plan kept the town's rules but missed a target the child wrote themselves. Ask: "Is your target possible here? Change the plan or change the target?"
  • A town-meeting choice broke a limit (ages 13–17). That is a real trade-off. Ask what they gained and what it cost; they can choose again.
A strong answer sounds like: "She was sure, but the flood lens showed water on the houses, so I told her dry land." By the end a child should be able to say what they checked, what the evidence showed and what they changed.

If a child types something worrying

Florence gives a fixed reply and suggests a trusted adult. Nothing typed is sent anywhere, so you will not be told: stay near enough to notice, and follow your organisation's safeguarding steps.
